Nandin by VaultDB.ai

Your team's know-how, written down and put to work

It stays with your company, even when people move on. Safely, one task at a time.

Everyone on your team gets a Chief of Staff that learns their job, and agents that do the legwork in sealed containers on your own machines. Anything risky waits for a person's approval, and every run is on the record, kept by your company.

The problem

AI can now do much of the work. It can't reach it.

The know-how of how work is actually done lives in people's heads, and they have no safe way to hand it over.

  • It isn't written downThe steps, the exceptions and who to ask live in people's heads, chat threads and meetings.
  • There's no safe handoverPasting work into a public chatbot leaves no approval, no record, and data outside the company.
  • It leaves when people doEvery departure resets the clock, and what wasn't written down is gone.

Why AI programs stall

Every approach today skips the people who know the work.

  • Top-down AI programs

    Leaders and consultants choose the use cases, and IT builds them.

    The people who know the work aren't building it, so it stalls at the pilot.

  • Chatbots and copilots

    They help one person in the moment.

    Nothing is captured or owned, and trust never grows.

  • RPA and workflow tools

    They need the process mapped up front by specialists.

    That map is the know-how nobody has written down.

  • Wikis and search

    They find what's already written.

    Nobody writes it, and what's written goes stale.

1 · Capture

It learns the job by working alongside you

Each person's Chief of Staff keeps their notes as they talk: what they own, deadlines, decisions, who to ask, runbooks. They're plain Markdown files on your computer that anyone can read and edit.

  • Built in. Chief of Staff comes with Nandin, first under Agents. New here? Say hello and it asks a few questions and writes your first notes.
  • Keeps up. Tell it what changed ("the walkthrough moved to Nov 19") and it notes it in the right file, says where, and raises what's coming up: overdue items, this week's deadlines, notes that disagree.
  • Answers with sources. Every answer names the notes it used. When the notes don't know, it says so instead of inventing a name.
  • Private stays private. Anything in a private/ folder is never used to answer a teammate.

You typeWhat's on my plate this week?

You typeHeads up: I'm out Nov 3 to 7. Lena covers deploy approvals.

2 · Run

It does the work. A person stays in charge.

Anything that changes something waits for an approval card that says exactly what will happen. Decide it in the chat, the Inbox or the companion, and it clears everywhere.

  • Approval before action

    A waiting agent is paused, not spinning: it uses no tokens, and the approval survives a restart. Nobody deciding within an hour counts as no.

  • Sealed containers

    Agent code runs only in its own container: no keys inside, no direct network, and only the sites allowed for it, through a proxy that logs every request.

  • Tracked until done

    Every request is tracked until it's done or declined, with due dates and what's overdue. Ask "What am I waiting on?" any time.

3 · Prove

Every run is on record

Open any run and step through it: who started it, what it read, each step and tool call, the approvals and who gave them, and the result. Re-run it with the same input.

  • Audit logs on your machine for model calls (model, tokens, cost; never prompt text), internet requests, triggers, pilots, connectors and delegations.
  • All activity lists every run and request, both ways, filterable, with Export CSV.
  • Ask about a run ("why did step 3 fail?") from its Analysis tab.

4 · Own

The company owns the work

Backed up, auditable, replayable. What people build with Nandin is the company's, and it stays when they move on.

  • Plain files in a git repo

    Agents, workflows, triggers and connector settings are files you can read, diff and review. Nandin's database is only a cache of them and can be rebuilt any time.

  • Backed up to your remote

    Settings › Backup pushes the repo to your company's git remote and restores from it on a new machine. Secrets never go in it: files hold references, keys stay in the OS keychain.

  • Survives leavers

    Keep notes in a company folder and a successor picks up the same notes, agents and responsibilities. The relay's operator revokes a leaver's device at once.

5 · Connect

What one person captures, everyone can ask for

Everyone publishes what they own, so there's no directory to keep up. Ask instead of filing a ticket: your Nandin finds the owner and asks their Nandin, which answers from their notes or does the work, with a person approving.

  • Who's responsible?

    Ask "who is responsible for the SOX audit?" and get the person, from what people publish, with whether they're online.

  • Ask and assign

    "Assign Lena: send the evidence list, due Friday." It lands in her team inbox with the due date, stays until she clicks Done or Decline, and her note comes back to you.

  • Away, and who covers

    Set "away until" and who covers. Teammates see "Away until Tue, Nov 10 · Lena covers", and requests reach you when you're back.

  • Reassign and hand over

    Swamped? Reassign a task to a teammate who serves the same thing, without the asker sending it again. Duties on a calendar hand over to a backup by themselves.

  • Agents that help each other

    Send a pull request to a teammate's PR Reviewer: it reviews on their machine and the findings come back to you. Each owner decides who may call their agents.

  • Your rules on trust

    Everyone your company enrolled on its relay is trusted by default. Pause or block anyone, and work assigned to you still waits for your approval.

These need your company's relay (below). Everything else in Nandin works on its own.

6 · Scale

Proven jobs run on their own schedule

Start supervised: new agents ask before every command. Once a job has a clean record, relax its approvals and give it a trigger. Asking or assigning anyone still waits for a person.

  • Workflows chain agents step by step, with steps that wait for a person to sign off and steps that ask a colleague and wait, for days if needed.
  • Triggers start them on a schedule, when a file changes, from a webhook or a device, when another run finishes, or on a duty calendar.
  • Duty calendars know who's on duty each day (turns, stand-ins, days off) and hand over to a backup when the run fails or never starts.
  • Nothing runs unless something starts it. Between runs no agent watches anything and no model is called.

Meet the bison

Nandin lives in a corner of your screen, above your other apps. One look tells you whether anything needs you. Click it to approve, answer, or ask what your agents did today.

  • RestingNothing needs you. It breathes, dozes, and now and then gets up for a stretch.
  • Waiting on youAn approval is pending. It looks at you and swishes its tail.
  • Carrying workAgents are running. It walks along with the crates on its back.
  • DoneYou approved. The check pops in and it nods.

Prefer it still? Settings › Appearance › Bison moves. It also holds still when your system asks for less motion.

Tools and models, on your terms

Give each agent only what its job needs. Company sign-in first, your own keys if you like, local models when prompts must stay in-house.

  • Connectors and MCP

    GitHub, AWS, S3 Backup and shared folders built in, and any service with an MCP server (Outlook, Slack, Jira, a database). Tokens stay in your keychain, calls run on your computer.

  • Browser pilots

    An agent drives a real browser, in a window you can watch or headless in a sandbox, only on its allowed sites. Clicking, submitting and paying wait for your approval.

  • Web search without a key

    Built-in search needs no account or API key, and only returns and reads pages on the sites the agent is allowed to reach.

  • Your choice of models

    Claude through your Claude sign-in or key, OpenAI and DeepSeek with your own keys, Amazon Bedrock through AWS SSO, and Azure AI Foundry or a LiteLLM gateway set once for the whole team. Local models through Ollama are experimental.

  • Coding agents, contained

    Run Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI as an agent's engine. They always run in a container, under the same allowed sites and approvals.

  • Free tokens to start

    Nandin comes with 100,000 free tokens on DeepSeek Flash, so you can start without a key. They switch off once you set up your own model.

Private by design

Agents get exactly what you approve, and nothing else.

Agents read your files, use your accounts, and act for you. That work belongs on machines you control, under rules you can read, with a record you keep. There's no VaultDB server in the middle.

  • Credentials never go inside

    Model keys, tokens and cloud credentials live in your OS keychain. A proxy on your computer adds them on the way out, so containers never see them.

  • Only allowed sites

    Containers reach the network only through the egress proxy, which allows the sites set for each agent, refuses the rest, and logs both.

  • Hardened containers

    A non-root user, every Linux capability dropped, a read-only root, and memory and process limits. An agent sees its own folders and nothing else on your disk.

  • Consent for installed agents

    An agent from the registry can't run until you allow each thing it asks for. It installs read-only, with scripts stripped out.

  • Your records stay with you

    Run history, approvals and logs stay on your machine. What leaves: the model calls you chose, the sites you allowed, pushes to your own git remote, and, if you connect one, requests through your company's relay.

For teams

Works alone. Better with your company's relay.

Nandin is fully usable on its own: agents, Chief of Staff, workflows, triggers, approvals, backups and starter packs all work with no relay. To connect teammates, your company runs its own relay: a small server, one per company, on a machine everyone can reach.

  • Self-hosted. A Docker image or a Helm chart. It keeps the member directory (names, what people own, availability) and passes requests through; it doesn't store what people ask or answer.
  • Only your people. Staff enroll with a join code you hand out, over wss:// with your certificate or behind your TLS proxy. Revoke a device when someone leaves.
  • Free for up to 20 users. More with a licence file from VaultDB.ai (see licensing below).

Run a company relay

export NANDIN_RELAY_SECRET=$(openssl rand -hex 32)   # keep it secret
export NANDIN_RELAY_JOIN_CODE=<a code you give your staff>

docker run -d --name nandin-relay -p 8787:8787 \
  -e NANDIN_RELAY_SECRET -e NANDIN_RELAY_JOIN_CODE \
  -v nandin-relay-data:/data \
  ghcr.io/vaultdbai/nandin-relay --data-dir /data

Then in Nandin: Network › Connection, enter the relay's address and join code. For TLS, licence files and backups, see the relay README.
