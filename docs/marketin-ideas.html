Yes, there is a very strong strategic point to proposing KRONOS Re even if a block of business is already reinsured. In the industry, this is often handled through a process called retrocession, but for a platform like KRONOS, the "point" is usually centered on arbitrage and efficiency.

Here is why you should still propose the system to a carrier or reinsurer who already has coverage:

1. Capturing the "Complexity Tax" Arbitrage

As noted in your KRONOS strategy, traditional reinsurers often charge a 50–100 basis point "Complexity Tax." * The Opportunity: If a carrier’s current reinsurer is using manual ALM and "sluggish regulatory reporting," they are likely holding overly conservative capital buffers. * The Proposal: You can propose that KRONOS Re takes over the block (via a novation or by being the new reinsurer when a treaty is recaptured) because your AI-native platform can model the risk more precisely. By reducing the "Uncertainty Buffer," you can offer the carrier better terms or a higher profit commission than their current "legacy" reinsurer.

2. Acting as a Retrocessionaire (Reinsurance for Reinsurers)

If you propose to the existing reinsurer rather than the original carrier, you are proposing a Retrocession deal. * Why they would say yes: Even big reinsurers (like Munich Re or Swiss Re) sometimes reach their "retention limit" or want to offload specific risks to free up their own capital. * The KRONOS Edge: You aren't just taking the risk; you are providing them with the "Digital Chief Actuary" dashboard. They get to offload the risk and they get access to your real-time Greeks and stochastic modeling to better monitor their remaining portfolio.

3. Treaty "Recapture" and Replacement

Reinsurance treaties are not always permanent. Many have "recapture" clauses that allow the original carrier to take the business back after a certain period (e.g., 10 years). * The Proposal: You propose KRONOS as the next destination. By showing off your end-to-end system and sample inforce loaders now, you position KRONOS as the obvious choice for when the current treaty expires or if the carrier decides to move the block to a more transparent, lower-cost provider.

4. The Sidecar "Capacity" Play

KRONOS is designed as a Bermuda Sidecar. Often, sidecars don't replace the main reinsurer; they sit alongside them to provide additional capacity. * The Proposal: "We know you have [Reinsurer X] for the bulk of this block, but KRONOS Re can act as a sidecar for the top 20% of the risk. Our AI-driven pricing allows us to be more surgical with the 'tail risk' that your main reinsurer might be overcharging you for."

5. Superior Transparency for PE Partners

If the "them" in your question is a Private Equity partner, they are often frustrated by the "black box" nature of traditional reinsurance. * The Point: Proposing a block that is already reinsured serves as a perfect benchmark. You can say: "Company X is currently paying 80bps in fees for this block. With our system, we can run it for 30bps and give you a real-time view of the assets. We can effectively 'refinance' this insurance risk."

Summary for your pitch: Don't view existing reinsurance as a "closed door." View it as a price to beat. If you can prove your system handles the ALM-to-Reporting loop more efficiently, you are proposing a way for them to "refinance" their risk at a lower cost of capital.
