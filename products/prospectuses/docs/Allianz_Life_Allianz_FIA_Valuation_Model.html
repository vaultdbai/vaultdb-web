Allianz Life Valuation Specifications

1. Scope

This document outlines the actuarial valuation specification for modeling Allianz Life Fixed Index Annuity (FIA) blocks, focusing on dominant products like the Allianz 222 and Allianz Benefit Control. These specifications are tailored for projecting stochastic cashflows and satisfying Bermuda Economic Balance Sheet (EBS) requirements.

2. Product Features & Mechanics

2.1 Accumulation Value (AV)

The account value grows based on fixed interest and indexed allocations. - Indexed Strategies: Include Annual Point-to-Point, Monthly Sum, and Two-Year Point-to-Point on various indices (S&P 500, Bloomberg US Dynamic Balance). - Growth drivers: Subject to declared Caps, Spreads, and Participation Rates. - Credit Logic: AV_t = AV_t-1 * (1 + max(0, min(Cap, Index_Return * Part_Rate - Spread)))

2.2 Protected Income Value (PIV) / GLWB Base

These products utilize a dual-pot system designed to emphasize income through a Guaranteed Lifetime Withdrawal Benefit (GLWB). - PIV Definition: A non-cash shadow account used strictly to calculate lifetime income. - Multiplier Rollup: Unlike fixed rollup rates, modern Allianz FIAs often apply a multiplier to the interest credited. E.g., The PIV is credited 150% or 250% of the dollar amount of interest credited to the AV. - Lifetime Income Plate: Annual_Income = PIV_at_activation * Payout_Percentage(Attained_Age). Some products offer increasing income options based on further index credits after activation.

2.3 Surrender & Death Benefits

  • Surrender Charge: Declining 10-year schedule (e.g. 10%, 10%, 9%, ..., 0%).
  • Market Value Adjustment (MVA): Applied on surrenders exceeding the free withdrawal amount during the surrender period.
  • Death Benefit: Standard is the greater of the AV or Minimum Guaranteed Surrender Value (MGSV).
  • Enhanced Death Benefit: Specific rider allows the full PIV to be paid out over a 5-year period instead of a lump sum of the AV.

3. Bermuda Regulatory Requirements (EBS Framework)

Under the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) specific regime, FIAs are evaluated using market-consistent principles.

3.1 Best Estimate Liability (BEL)

The BEL is the probability-weighted average of present-value cashflows (Benefits + Expenses) over 1,000+ stochastic risk-neutral or real-world economic scenarios. - Moneyness Calculation: GLWB optionality requires intrinsic modeling. The rider is "in the money" (ITM) when the actuarial present value of the lifetime income stream exceeds the AV. - Discount Curve: Derived via the Scenario Based Approach (SBA). A matching adjustment based on the actual asset portfolio spread replaces the standard risk-free rate, significantly reducing the liability.

3.2 Risk Margin (RM)

Bermuda requires calculating a Risk Margin using a Cost of Capital (CoC) approach (fixed at 6%). - Modeled by projecting the Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR) for non-hedgeable risks (Base Lapse, Mortality, Longevity, Expenses) over the run-off of the block and discounting back.

4. Actuarial Modeling Framework (Python Implementation Specs)

4.1 Dynamic Behavioral Lapses

Lapse rates must react dynamically to the economic environment and the value of guarantees. Lapse_Rate(t) = Base_Lapse(t) * f(ITM) Where f(ITM) scales down the lapse towards a floor (e.g., 2%-3%) when the GLWB is deeply in the money.

4.2 Dynamic Income Utilization

Modelers should employ transition matrices or cohort delay functions. - Trigger: Once past the waiting period (e.g., 10 years or age 60), if ITM > 1.2, an accelerated utilization vector is applied.

4.3 Stochastic Indexation Module

  • An equity generation module must project index returns, dividend yields, and volatility continuously.
  • Hedging limits: Cap/Par rate setting models must be dynamically simulated based on the prevailing option budget (usually defined by fixed income yield - spread).
